Why this matters

A shop that runs perfectly still looks invisible to a bank.

Small merchants and informal businesses run their whole operation from memory, a paper notebook, and a phone. Credit handed to regular customers goes untracked, so money quietly leaks out of the till every week.

When the same merchant applies for a loan, there's no income to show — because most business software assumes a desktop and comfortable English, neither of which describes the counter of a neighbourhood store.

KhataSaathi is built the other way round: mobile-first, voice-first, and vernacular by default — a toolkit for the merchant's whole financial life, not just one narrow task.

The toolkit

Six modules. One merchant. Zero accounting background required.

Every feature answers the challenge directly — a mobile-first, vernacular toolkit that makes payments, bookkeeping, credit access and basic planning practical at the counter of a small shop.

Voice & vernacular entry

Record a sale, an expense or a credit note by speaking in a local language. KhataSaathi reads it back for confirmation in the same language before it ever touches the books.

Khata digitisation

Track exactly who owes what. Send a polite, native-language reminder in one tap and record part-payments as they trickle in.

Automatic books

Every voice entry rolls up into a simple daily profit view and a monthly summary — a document a lender can actually read, no accountant needed.

Credit readiness

A live score shows the merchant exactly what a lender would see today, and precisely what's missing from the file to improve it.

Low-bandwidth design

Works fully offline on an entry-level phone. Entries queue locally and sync the moment a signal returns — nothing is ever lost.

Assisted onboarding

A family member or shop assistant can set the account up together — with sensitive controls like withdrawals kept locked to the owner.

Cash-flow seasonality Bonus creativity

A simple week-by-week view tells the merchant which weeks of the year their cash historically runs tight — so restocking and repayments can be timed around it, not against it.

Under the hood

From a spoken sentence to a lender-ready file.

Three quiet steps run behind every screen you just tried.

01 — Capture

Listen, in the merchant's own words

On-device speech capture works offline, in the shop's chosen language, and queues the raw entry the instant it's spoken — no signal required.

02 — Understand

Turn speech into structure

Each entry is parsed into customer, item, amount and type, shown back in plain language for a one-tap confirmation before anything is saved.

03 — Act

Compound it into leverage

Confirmed entries roll into books, a credit score, reminders and a seasonality view — the everyday record becomes the paperwork a lender trusts.
